# Evidence-Derived Guardrails

These are **working research guardrails**, not timeless doctrine or final Southbrook strategy. They reflect current research in the Brain and should be revised if stronger evidence emerges.

## 1. Activity is not maturity

Attendance, serving, group participation, giving, and program completion are useful signals/environments but cannot carry a discipleship assessment alone. Use layered evidence including formation, mission, disciple-making, multiplication, stories, and pastoral discernment.

## 2. Multiplication must name the unit

“Four generations” is meaningless without identifying what reproduced: individual disciple-makers, leaders, groups, microchurches, churches/plants, or another unit. Track consistent generations where possible.

## 3. Early participation and maturing authority are different questions

Current research supports involving newer believers early in witness, invitation, Scripture-reading, simple practice, and supervised facilitation while increasing weightier teaching/pastoral responsibility with demonstrated character, doctrine, wisdom, and faithfulness.

## 4. Practice needs coaching

Disciple-making appears to be learned through modeled and supported practice more than classroom information alone. Training should move toward observation, participation, coached action, correction, and eventual reproduction.

## 5. Simplicity helps reproduction; simplicity alone does not guarantee fidelity

Tools must be understandable and reproducible by ordinary believers. Oversimplification can also lose theological/relational depth, so coaching, reference checking, healthy church oversight, and formation matter.

## 6. G1→G2 deserves special attention

A recurring research question is the transition from receiving discipleship to intentionally discipling another person. Systems should explicitly test whether identity, invitation, confidence, modeling, practice, coaching, and reproduction expectation are present.

## 7. Self-reported movement claims require caution

Large movement numbers can inspire research but should not become project facts without clear definitions, provenance, and appropriate verification.

## 8. Programs do not transfer; principles may

Always move: `PRACTICE → MECHANISM → PRINCIPLE → LOCAL APPLICATION`.
