# Research ↔ Practice ↔ Local Learning Framework

Brain companion document: `https://docs.google.com/document/d/1qo-2MbcDDczpli87-koONL7-B8JOWMiAe6CoYSRmKdw/edit`

## Purpose

The Discipleship Brain exists to help Southbrook **make faithful, practical, learnable decisions about forming disciples who make disciples**, not merely to accumulate high-confidence research conclusions.

The system therefore uses two complementary operating modes:

1. **RESEARCH / TRUTH MODE** — What appears to be true, how confident are we, and why?
2. **PRACTICE / DESIGN MODE** — What are credible churches, movements, practitioners, books, and historical examples actually doing; why might those practices work; which are worth contextualizing and testing at Southbrook?

Neither mode replaces the other.

Research without practice can become sterile. Practice without research can become anecdote, marketing, or imitation.

## Core distinction

Do not require the same evidence threshold for:

- declaring a universal or durable principle;
- considering a practice;
- running a reversible small test;
- launching a 3–6 month pilot;
- redesigning a church-wide system;
- scaling a practice broadly.

**Evidence required should rise with risk, cost, scale, irreversibility, theological/pastoral consequence, and difficulty of reversal.**

A practice can be sufficiently warranted for a small test without being empirically proven as a causal discipleship intervention.

## Seven lenses for practice judgment

Keep these separate:

1. **Biblical / theological coherence** — Is the practice faithful to Scripture and sound pastoral theology?
2. **Direct effect evidence** — Is there credible evidence the exact practice produces the desired outcome?
3. **Mechanism evidence** — Is the underlying mechanism supported by theology, learning science, behavior science, mentoring research, implementation research, organizational research, or other relevant evidence?
4. **Practitioner precedent** — Is the practice used repeatedly by credible churches/movements/practitioners, and what do they report learning?
5. **Transferability** — What depends on context, culture, scale, leadership, demographics, persecution, movement conditions, or denominational assumptions?
6. **Southbrook fit** — Does the practice address a real local bottleneck and fit our people, capacity, ministries, culture, and leadership reality?
7. **Local learning** — What happened when Southbrook actually tried it, with what people, support, fidelity, outcomes, side effects, and reproduction?

Do not collapse these into one generic “evidence score.”

## Practice-status vocabulary

Use practice status separately from project-confidence vocabulary.

- **OBSERVED PRACTICE** — a credible source documents that someone actually does it.
- **REPEATED PRACTICE PATTERN** — meaningfully similar practice appears across independent contexts.
- **PROMISING PRACTICE** — plausible mechanism + credible precedent + no major contradiction; direct effect evidence may still be limited.
- **SMALL-TEST CANDIDATE** — promising enough for a low-risk reversible experiment in the local context.
- **PILOT CANDIDATE** — sufficient operational clarity, local relevance, support, and measurement to justify a bounded pilot.
- **LOCALLY SUPPORTED PRACTICE** — Southbrook has meaningful local learning suggesting the practice helps under stated conditions.
- **INTEGRATION / SCALE CANDIDATE** — repeated local learning, capacity, fidelity, reproducibility, and strategic fit justify broader consideration.
- **NOT NOW / POOR FIT** — practice may be useful elsewhere but is currently mismatched, too risky, too staff-dependent, too unclear, or not addressing the local problem.

These labels do **not** mean “proven true.”

## Action-warrant ladder

### 1. EXPLORE

Appropriate when:
- the practice is relevant and sourceable;
- the mechanism is plausible;
- uncertainty is still substantial.

Action:
- study operational details;
- find counterexamples;
- compare alternatives.

### 2. SMALL TEST

Appropriate when:
- biblical/theological fit is acceptable;
- mechanism is plausible;
- local problem is real;
- cost/risk is low;
- practice is reversible;
- measurement can be simple.

Direct causal evidence for the exact church practice is **not required**.

### 3. BOUNDED PILOT

Appropriate when:
- practice is operationally clear;
- there is credible precedent or mechanism support;
- local fit is plausible;
- coaching/support is available;
- risks are bounded;
- learning criteria are defined.

### 4. INTEGRATION CANDIDATE

Appropriate when:
- the pilot produced useful local evidence;
- the practice fits existing ministries or a desired future system;
- capacity and ownership are plausible;
- unintended effects are understood enough to proceed cautiously.

### 5. SCALE CANDIDATE

Appropriate when:
- local evidence is repeated or sustained;
- people can reproduce the practice without unusual staff dependence;
- fidelity and theological depth are maintained;
- the practice contributes to the North Star;
- costs/risks are acceptable;
- leadership has made the relevant decision.

## Research as backbone, not gatekeeper

For practical ministry questions, empirical research often supports **mechanisms** more strongly than named church programs.

Examples:

- apprenticeship may be supported by modeling, deliberate practice, coaching, transfer, and increasing responsibility;
- testimony practice may be supported by retrieval, rehearsal, narrative identity, and social confidence;
- family table practices may be supported by habit formation, parent modeling, relational warmth, cueing, and low-friction implementation.

Use those evidence streams to interrogate and strengthen a practice.

Do not say:
> “There is no randomized church study, therefore this practice is not worth trying.”

Also do not say:
> “Several churches do it, therefore it works.”

Use:
`PRACTICE → OBSERVED OUTCOME → MECHANISM → RESEARCH BACKBONE → TRANSFERABILITY → LOCAL FIT → TEST → LOCAL LEARNING`

## Knowledge architecture

### Living Synthesis

Stores the project's evolving conclusions about mechanisms/principles.

Do not turn it into a catalog of every ministry practice.

### Practice Intelligence Library

Stores concrete external practices and comparisons:

`04 — RESEARCH INTELLIGENCE → 06 Practice Intelligence Library`

Subfolders:

- `01 Practice Cards`
- `02 Practice Comparisons & Landscapes`

### Southbrook Diagnostic

Stores what is actually happening locally and the bottlenecks/opportunities practices should address.

### Experiments, Pilots & Learning

Stores Southbrook tests, results, adaptations, and local evidence.

Completed/in-progress pilots should be interpreted through `discipleship-pilot-learning-evaluator` so implementation fidelity, burden, outcomes, stories, and reproduction are assessed before raising the local action warrant.

### Strategy & Decisions

Stores recommendations, tradeoffs, approved decisions, rationale, and sequencing.

## Default practical reasoning sequence

`LOCAL PROBLEM → PRACTICE LANDSCAPE → OPERATIONAL DETAIL → MECHANISM → EVIDENCE BACKBONE → TRANSFERABILITY → SOUTHBROOK FIT → ACTION WARRANT → TEST/PILOT → IMPLEMENT SUPPORT → PILOT LEARNING → ADAPT/INTEGRATE/SCALE/STOP`

## North Star test

For every practice ask:

- Does it help people know/love Jesus and abide in Christ?
- Does it cultivate obedience, character, and ownership?
- Does it move ordinary people toward mission?
- Does it help someone intentionally help another follow Jesus?
- Can the recipient eventually reproduce it?
- Can ordinary believers carry it without excessive staff dependence?
- Is it understandable, practicable, coachable, and teachable?
- Could it become normal across the church if local learning supports it?

## Anti-patterns

Avoid:

- evidence paralysis;
- copying famous churches;
- ranking practices by popularity;
- requiring universal proof before a small reversible test;
- scaling a promising practice without local learning;
- treating practitioner claims as independent evidence;
- ignoring theology because a practice “works”;
- using local anecdotes as universal truth;
- mistaking a polished curriculum for a multiplication mechanism.
