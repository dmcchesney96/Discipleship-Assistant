# Discipleship Research PDF Standard

## Purpose

This is the reusable visual and information-design standard for long-form research reports in the Discipleship Brain.

The desired feel is:

- calm;
- serious;
- modern;
- pastor-readable;
- highly skimmable;
- evidence-aware;
- more like a professionally designed research brief than a chat export.

Retain useful editorial structure: generous white space, a prominent evidence-weighted answer, numbered sections, callouts, and simple comparisons. Apply **Southbrook Church's visual identity** through `southbrook-brand-artifact-stylist` and [the shared production workflow](southbrook-document-build-workflow.md), including observed header/text patterns. This standard owns information architecture, not a competing visual brand.

## 1. Page format

- Default: US Letter, portrait.
- Long reports: usually 12–40 pages; do not pad to hit a page count.
- Body margins: approximately 0.65–0.8 inches.
- Keep a stable text measure; avoid full-width dense paragraphs.
- Use generous vertical spacing between ideas.
- Avoid columns for normal body text. Two-column layouts are acceptable only for compact comparisons or callouts.

## 2. Visual system

### Color palette

Use the verified Southbrook palette with strong contrast:

- Body/cover background: white or near-white `#F7F9FA`
- Primary ink: print charcoal `#231F20`
- Identity accent and rules: cyan `#00AEEF`
- Dark title/answer panel: navy `#002A40`, with white text
- Light callout: pale cyan `#E5F7FD`, with dark text
- Metadata: a readable gray from the brand profile

Keep the document usable in grayscale. Evidence/status distinctions must remain explicit in text.

### Typography

Use the Southbrook sans-serif hierarchy: licensed Gotham where legitimately available, otherwise Arial for editable masters and exports. Use bold headings, short differentiated labels, and generous spacing. Do not introduce serif headlines as a separate report identity. Adjust the sizes below for readability and document role.

Recommended scale:

- Cover title: 30–44 pt
- Section title: 22–30 pt
- Finding title: 14–18 pt
- Body: 9.5–11.5 pt
- Labels / running heads: 7.5–9 pt
- Source appendix: 8.5–10 pt

Do not shrink body type to fit too much content.

## 3. Cover page

The cover should include:

Top line:
- `DISCIPLESHIP RESEARCH REPORT`
- date at upper-right

Middle:
- short topic eyebrow
- main title
- descriptive subtitle / research question

Primary callout:
- label: `EVIDENCE-WEIGHTED ANSWER`
- 1–3 sentence answer
- one small qualifier/limitation line when needed

Footer:
- `Southbrook Church | Discipleship Brain Research`
- page 1 / total

The cover should have enough empty space to feel intentional.

## 4. Start Here / Executive Summary page

This is the highest-value page.

Layout:

- running header: short report title | EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
- thin accent rule
- section label `01 / EXECUTIVE SUMMARY`
- one large synthesis statement
- one short explanatory paragraph
- 3–6 numbered findings separated by rules
- one larger synthesis sentence / pull quote
- `EVIDENCE BOUNDARY` callout

The reader should understand the report's core answer from this page alone.

## 5. Section pages

Each major section begins with:

- running header;
- thin divider;
- accent rule;
- numbered section label;
- strong Southbrook sans-serif headline;
- 1–3 sentence section lead.

Use numbered sections consistently:

`01 / EXECUTIVE SUMMARY`  
`02 / DEFINITIONS`  
`03 / BIBLICAL & THEOLOGICAL ANALYSIS`  
etc.

Do not restart numbering for subsections.

## 6. Finding blocks

A major finding should normally include:

### [Finding title]
`HIGH CONFIDENCE` or other confidence badge

**What the evidence says**  
1–2 concise paragraphs with claim-level citations.

**Why this may happen**  
Mechanism explanation.

**Important caution**  
What the evidence does not establish / contrary evidence.

**Why this matters**  
Only when a ministry implication is genuinely useful.

Avoid making every finding a full-page card.

## 7. Callout styles

### Evidence-weighted answer
Deep navy panel, white text.

### Evidence boundary
Pale cyan or neutral panel with a thin cyan rule and dark text.

### Important caution / do not overclaim
Light neutral panel with a clear charcoal caution label and restrained rule.

### Southbrook implication
Light neutral panel. Label clearly:
`SOUTHBROOK IMPLICATION — HYPOTHESIS`
or
`SOUTHBROOK QUESTION`

### Brain change
Light cyan/neutral panel:
`WHAT CHANGED IN THE BRAIN`

Keep callouts concise. They should help scanning, not interrupt every page.

## 8. Confidence badges

Use small uppercase labels:

- HIGH CONFIDENCE
- PROMISING
- CONTEXT DEPENDENT
- UNCERTAIN
- CONTRADICTED

Use dark navy/charcoal text on white, pale cyan, or neutral fills, with modest rules/weight for hierarchy. Preserve the explicit label instead of inventing a separate status palette.

Do not rely on color alone. Always include the text label.

## 9. Citations next to claims

The report uses source keys.

Examples:

`[S1]`  
`[S2; S5]`  
`[S7, pp. 44–48]`

Place the source key directly after the supported sentence or table value.

Do not force the reader to infer which paragraph a source supports.

At the end of the report, provide a numbered/keyed source ledger with full citations and direct links.

### Source-ledger entry

`S7 — Author. Title. Publisher/Journal/Organization, Year. Source type. Direct link.`

Add a short provenance note only when it changes how the reader should interpret the source:

- organizational self-report;
- secondary summary;
- abstract only;
- archived webpage;
- independently verified dataset.

## 10. Tables

Use a light editorial style:

- minimal borders;
- header row with soft panel fill;
- left-aligned text;
- numeric columns aligned consistently;
- enough padding to scan;
- source keys in relevant cells or a source column.

Avoid tiny landscape tables unless no reasonable alternative exists.

If a table would require body text below 8.5 pt, redesign it.

## 11. Diagrams

Use only to make a concept easier to understand.

Good uses:

- Practice → Mechanism → Principle
- maturity / responsibility continuum
- generational multiplication chain
- evidence-strength ladder
- pathway / feedback loop
- comparison of models

Diagrams should be simple enough to understand in under 20 seconds.

## 12. First-three-pages rule

By page 3, the reader should know:

- the question;
- short answer;
- most important findings;
- confidence;
- main limitation;
- why the research matters.

Methodology can begin after the answer is clear.

## 13. Required ending sections

### What this research does not prove

Concise bullets/paragraphs.

### What changed in the Discipleship Brain

Show:

- prior conclusion;
- new evidence;
- change classification;
- updated conclusion/confidence;
- unresolved tension;
- Southbrook status.

### Sources

Keyed source ledger.

### Optional appendix

- claim register;
- verification table;
- detailed case matrix;
- method details.

Do not make the claim register the main reading experience.

## 14. Running furniture

Body pages should include:

Header:
- abbreviated report title on left
- section title on right

Footer:
- `Research synthesis • [date]`
- page X / Y

Keep header/footer subtle.

## 15. Accessibility / readability

- Do not use color as the only signal.
- Maintain high contrast.
- Avoid all-caps body paragraphs.
- Break long URLs across lines only in the source appendix.
- Use real heading styles in the editable master.
- Add descriptive alt text to meaningful figures when the authoring format supports it.
- Keep table header rows marked as headers when possible.

## 16. PDF quality-control standard

The report is not finished when export succeeds.

Required:

1. export/convert to PDF;
2. render all pages to images;
3. inspect visually;
4. fix clipping, overlap, broken glyphs, awkward table splits, blank pages, stranded headings, footer collisions, and tiny citations;
5. re-export;
6. render again if fixes were needed.

No final PDF should contain:

- clipped text;
- overlapping text;
- black/missing-glyph squares;
- broken page numbering;
- tables cut off the page;
- headings alone at the bottom of a page;
- unreadably small sources;
- missing source ledger.

## 17. File naming

`YYYY-MM-DD — [Short Research Title] — Discipleship Research.pdf`

Editable master:

`YYYY-MM-DD — [Short Research Title] — Discipleship Research.docx`

Use ASCII hyphens instead of special dashes if a toolchain has filename problems.

## 18. What not to do

- Do not export raw chat transcript styling.
- Do not begin with a long methodology section.
- Do not make every paragraph a colored card.
- Do not use stock photography.
- Do not put decorative icons everywhere.
- Do not use gradients, glossy effects, or 3D charts.
- Do not leave claims uncited when the source ledger supports them.
- Do not include a bibliography without claim-level source keys in the body.
- Do not visually imply certainty that the evidence does not support.
