# Source Hierarchy

Use the strongest available source that fits the question. This is a preference order, not a mechanical ranking.

## External research

Prefer:

1. Scripture and serious biblical/theological scholarship for theological claims.
2. Peer-reviewed research, longitudinal studies, strong datasets, rigorous surveys.
3. Primary church/ministry/network documents, manuals, training materials, annual reports, measurement reports.
4. Seminary/denominational research and credible church-research organizations.
5. Books and substantive practitioner interviews from people close to the work.
6. Credible case studies and conference presentations.
7. Secondary analysis that adds real synthesis.
8. AI-generated reports only as research leads/input.

A primary organizational source is authoritative for what the organization says/does; it is not automatically independent proof that the model works.

## Southbrook-local truth

For current operational reality, prefer:

1. Explicit current approved decisions/policies.
2. Current system records and canonical documents.
3. Direct current ministry evidence/data.
4. Multiple converging interviews/observations.
5. Single interviews/observations.
6. Historical documents.
7. Memory/inference.

Resolve conflicts by authority, recency, specificity, and provenance. If still unresolved, preserve the conflict.

## User instruction

The user's current explicit instruction outranks older project artifacts when they conflict, unless doing so would violate safety, privacy, or legal constraints.
