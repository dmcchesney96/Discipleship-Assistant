## Required source citations: Turabian footnotes (Dan's standing rule, November 20, 2026)

This applies whenever Dan selects a source-derived learning, truth, principle, or conviction for the Brain, including a resource insight he restates in his own words. It is required for new captures and for substantive updates to existing captures, not just for research reports.

- Reopen the original source in the Brain before saving. Cite the actual author or organization, exact work/title, edition and publication information when available, and printed page(s), chapter, section, or other precise locator. Retain a stable link to the Brain copy and, if available, the original publication in the footnote itself (prefer hyperlinking the cited title to the Brain copy, and include original publication URL where appropriate). A link alone or a generic source list is not a citation. Use footnotes as the sole formal citation apparatus for internal selected-learning records: do not also print a full source citation, citation list, standalone source link, or repeated bibliography in the body. Readable author/work headings are fine when helpful; they are not a second bibliographic entry. Consolidate closely related attributed claims under one note when the attribution remains clear.
- Use Turabian notes-bibliography style, with numbered superscript footnote markers immediately after each direct quotation, attributed paraphrase, or source-dependent claim. Use real native Google Docs footnotes when supported, and verify their contents and marker placement on readback. If native footnotes cannot be created or verified, use numbered superscript markers and a clearly labeled Footnotes section in Turabian format; report that fallback in the final response. Do not silently substitute bare URLs or author-date references.
- Use a full first note for each distinct source and a shortened author/title/page note for subsequent references. Book example: Firstname Lastname, *Book Title* (City: Publisher, Year), 23–24. Journal example: Firstname Lastname, “Article Title,” *Journal Name* volume, no. issue (year): specific page(s), DOI or URL. Web example: Firstname Lastname, “Page Title,” Organization or Site, Month Day, Year, URL. Use the appropriate Turabian form for the actual resource type, including an access date for undated online material when needed. Do not invent missing dates, publishers, editions, page numbers, authors, or URLs; record the available metadata and identify any unverified locator.
- Distinguish an author's direct quotation from paraphrase, Dan's endorsement/own formulation, and assistant-created wording. Cite the source that actually supports the idea, not the assistant synthesis as if it were an author's quotation. For Scripture, use book, chapter, and verse and specify the translation when quoting; do not fabricate an external source for Dan's original thought. Where a source is only available through a secondary quotation, explicitly mark “quoted in” and cite the source actually consulted.
- For a learning or conviction developed from several explicitly selected or approved sources, keep one canonical record and attach each approved source at the specific wording it supports. Organize source contributions with readable headings, the complete selected excerpt and explanation, and how it supports, deepens, qualifies, or differs from the conviction. Keep bibliographic metadata and locators in footnotes, not repeated in body-text citation lines. Distinguish converging views from disagreement; do not attribute the combined formulation to a single author.
