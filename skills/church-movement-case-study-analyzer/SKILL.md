# Church & Movement Case Study Analyzer

## Core questions

### Definitions
How do they define disciple, discipleship, maturity, disciple-maker, multiplication, and success?

### Pathway
Map: `ENTRY → FORMATION → COMMUNITY → MISSION → LEADERSHIP → MULTIPLICATION`.

### Actual practice
Who disciples whom? What happens weekly/monthly? One-to-one, triads, groups, missional communities, coaching, Bible/prayer, evangelism, apprenticeships, family systems?

Recover enough operational detail to understand the practice:
- who initiates;
- exact steps;
- cadence/duration;
- group/relationship size;
- tools/materials;
- training before launch;
- coaching/check-ins;
- maturity/authority threshold;
- theological/pastoral oversight;
- what happens when someone struggles/fails;
- staff/volunteer load;
- how the practice changes as people mature;
- how reproduction is expected/recognized.

### Ordinary-person expectation
What is an ordinary participant expected and equipped to do? When do they begin practicing? What requires higher maturity/authority?

### Leadership system
How are disciple-makers identified, trained, observed, coached, released, corrected, and supported? How are leaders reproduced?

### Evangelism connection
How do people far from God enter relationships, hear/respond to gospel, and move into formation?

### Multiplication mechanism
What behavior/system plausibly causes another generation to emerge? Trace G0→G1→G2→G3→G4+ only when the unit is consistent and evidence supports it.

### Evidence
Separate:
- documented evidence;
- organizational claims;
- practitioner opinion;
- interpretation;
- hypothesis.

Look for independent corroboration, longitudinal persistence, internally developed leaders, reproducing disciple-makers, relational evangelism, group/leader reproduction, and sustained practices.

### Failure/limits
What has failed, stalled, been criticized, or depended on unique context? What theological, cultural, size, staffing, or movement conditions matter?

## Synthesis frame

For every notable practice:

`PRACTICE → OBSERVED/REPORTED OUTCOME → PLAUSIBLE MECHANISM → RESEARCH BACKBONE → TRANSFERABLE PRINCIPLE → TRANSFERABILITY LIMIT → LOCAL APPLICATION OPTIONS`

For "what they do" claims, primary organizational material can be strong practice evidence even when outcome evidence is weak. Keep those evidentiary questions separate.

Do not conclude “Church X does this, so Southbrook should.”

## Output

- Case snapshot/context
- Definitions
- Pathway/system map
- Practices and leadership/coaching system
- Multiplication mechanism
- Evidence matrix with confidence
- Failure modes/risks
- Transferable principles
- Context-specific practices
- **Practice Card handoff(s)** using `templates/discipleship-practice-card-template.md` for practices worth preserving
- Southbrook questions/hypotheses (not prescriptions)
- Research gaps / sources

Route reusable cards to `discipleship-practice-intelligence-builder` / Practice Intelligence Library. Do not bury operational practice learning only inside the case report.

## Descriptive method studies

For a practical church-methods question, foreground actual roles, steps, cadence, tools, training, feedback, burden and support. Scope generational analysis to relevant claims rather than requiring every method to document G4. Separate prescribed instructions, reported use, observed enactment and outcomes. Preserve unknown fields; missing documentation does not prove missing practice. Route operational verification to `discipleship-practice-source-verifier`.
