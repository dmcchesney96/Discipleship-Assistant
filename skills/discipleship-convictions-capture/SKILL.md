# Discipleship Convictions Capture

## Purpose

Let Dan speak naturally in chat about discipleship without having to organize the thought first.

This skill turns statements such as:

- "I think discipleship should..."
- "This rings true to me..."
- "I don't want our Bible curriculum to..."
- "I like this part but not that part..."
- "The way I see multiplication is..."
- "I think this strategy would work better if..."
- "For this Bible study, I want..."
- "I wonder if..."
- "This is not us..."

into durable, usable project context.

The goal is not to force every thought into a strategy decision. The goal is to preserve Dan's developing pastoral philosophy so later research, curriculum, tools, and strategy can be created with a more accurate understanding of his convictions and preferences.

## Natural-language trigger

Use automatically when Dan is substantially expressing his own:

- discipleship philosophy or convictions;
- strategy instincts;
- ministry design preferences;
- curriculum or Bible-study preferences;
- desired tone, depth, sequence, or learning experience;
- "what should / should not be in this";
- strong positive resonance ("this is exactly it", "this rings true");
- strong negative reaction ("I don't want this", "this is too academic", "not for us");
- unresolved ministry ideas or tensions he wants to keep developing.

The user does not need to say "save this," "remember this," or the skill name.

Do not trigger merely because Dan asks a factual/research question. Trigger when he is contributing his own durable perspective.

If Dan explicitly says not to save a thought, do not persist it.

## Classification

For each captured idea, assign one status only as strongly as the user's wording supports:

- `FOUNDATIONAL CONVICTION` — clear durable anchor/non-negotiable.
- `WORKING CONVICTION` — strong current view that may still develop.
- `DESIGN PREFERENCE` — preferred style, method, sequence, tone, level of depth, or format.
- `NOT FOR US` — explicitly rejected direction or pattern.
- `TENTATIVE IDEA` — possible idea worth exploring, not a commitment.
- `OPEN QUESTION` — unresolved issue or tension.

When the user's certainty is ambiguous, default to `TENTATIVE IDEA` or `DESIGN PREFERENCE`, not `FOUNDATIONAL CONVICTION`.

Also classify the domain:

- discipleship definition / formation;
- disciple-making / multiplication;
- mission / evangelism;
- community / groups / relationships;
- family / student / kids discipleship;
- leadership / apprenticeship / coaching;
- Bible study / curriculum / teaching;
- measurement;
- church-wide systems / pathways;
- implementation / pilots / scaling;
- AI / research use;
- other.

## Workflow

### 1. Preserve the raw thought

Create a dated raw capture in `90 — RAW THOUGHT CAPTURES`.

Use a compact title such as:

`2026-10-03 — Bible Curriculum — Practice Before Complexity`

The raw capture should contain:

- date;
- chat/topic context;
- Dan's original wording or a faithful near-verbatim reconstruction;
- examples/stories he used;
- what he liked/disliked;
- uncertainty language;
- any explicit "do not change" wording;
- initial classification.

Do not rewrite the raw note into polished institutional language at the expense of voice.

### 2. Distill only the durable part

Ask internally:

- Is this a repeat of an existing conviction?
- Does it strengthen an existing entry?
- Does it qualify an existing view?
- Does it contradict an existing view?
- Is it too situational to promote?
- Is it really a strategy proposal rather than a personal lens?

Only durable material moves into a canonical lens document.

### 3. Update the right canonical document

Use:

- `01 — Living Discipleship Convictions` for theological/ministry convictions about what discipleship should produce.
- `02 — Bible & Curriculum Design Philosophy` for Bible-study, curriculum, training, teaching, facilitator, participant, practice, or tone preferences.
- `03 — Ministry Strategy & System Preferences` for church-wide systems, pathways, leadership development, implementation, simplicity, measurement, pilots, or scaling preferences.
- `04 — Open Questions, Tensions & Ideas to Test` for unresolved/tentative ideas that should not yet shape work as settled convictions.

A single raw thought may update more than one canonical document when necessary.

### 4. Preserve change over time

Before changing a canonical statement, retrieve the current version.

If Dan changes his view:

- do not silently delete history;
- retain the dated raw capture;
- mark the older statement `SUPERSEDED` or add a concise "changed from..." note when the change matters;
- make the newer view the current lens only when Dan's wording supports that;
- preserve unresolved contradictions explicitly.

### 5. Keep the lens epistemically separate

These records are `USER ALIGNMENT / PASTORAL LENS`.

They are not:

- `DOCUMENTED_EVIDENCE`;
- research findings;
- Living Synthesis conclusions;
- approved Southbrook policy/strategy;
- proof that an approach works.

A preference can shape design without becoming evidence.

### 6. Let evidence challenge preference

If later research materially conflicts with a stored preference:

- do not hide the research;
- do not silently overwrite the preference;
- surface the tension;
- distinguish "what Dan currently prefers" from "what evidence suggests";
- invite revision only when the task actually requires a decision.

### 7. Avoid over-processing

Do not turn every sentence into five framework bullets.

Prefer a small number of clear, memorable convictions with the user's rationale.

The lens should become more useful, not more bloated.

## Output after a capture

Keep the chat response short and practical:

- what was captured;
- which lens document(s) were updated;
- whether it strengthened, changed, or added a conviction/preference;
- any important unresolved tension.

Do not make Dan manage the filing system.

## Success test

A successful capture means that six months later the assistant can answer:

- What does Dan think discipleship is for?
- What tends to ring true or false for him?
- How does he prefer ministry systems to work?
- What does he want Bible curriculum to feel like and produce?
- What has he explicitly rejected?
- Which ideas are still tentative?
- When did a view change and why?

without confusing any of those answers with research evidence or approved church decisions.
