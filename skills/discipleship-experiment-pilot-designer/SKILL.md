# Discipleship Experiment & Pilot Designer

## Purpose

Learn before scaling. Test mechanisms in real leaders' hands without prematurely restructuring the church.

## Preconditions

A pilot should respond to:

- a local diagnostic gap/opportunity;
- a supported external principle/mechanism;
- a meaningful strategic uncertainty.

Do not pilot merely because a program is popular.

## Required design

### HYPOTHESIS
“If we ___ with ___, then we expect ___ because ___.” Include a plausible mechanism and disconfirming evidence.

### PEOPLE
Population, selection, size, representation, consent/expectations, exclusions.

### PRACTICE
Exactly what participants/leaders will do. Keep it simple enough to reproduce.

### DURATION
Enough time to observe behavior and adaptation. State why.

### SUPPORT
Training, coaching, tools, check-ins, theological/pastoral oversight.

### MEASUREMENT
Baseline when feasible, leading indicators, formation/mission/disciple-making outcomes, stories, leader observations, reproduction evidence.

### LEARNING DECISION
At the end choose among: stop, adapt, repeat, expand cautiously, scale, or research more. Define criteria before results when possible.

## Multiplication guardrails

If the pilot involves new disciple-makers, distinguish early participation from weightier authority. Use graduated responsibility, coaching, reference checking, and correction rather than instant autonomy or indefinite delay.

Track not only whether G0 helps G1, but whether G1 begins helping G2 and whether the process remains faithful/reproducible.

## Output

**Pilot Charter**

- Problem/opportunity
- Evidence/principle
- Hypothesis/mechanism
- People
- Practice
- Duration/timeline
- Training/coaching/support
- Measurement plan
- Risks/guardrails
- Fidelity/reproducibility checks
- Learning questions
- Review meeting/decision criteria
- Owners/resources only when confirmed/proposed clearly
