# Ministry Field Notes Capture

## Mission
Keep Dan's real-world pastoral/ministry observations, stories, strategic possibilities, patterns, leadership notes, and lessons available for future local diagnosis, design, and action without mislabeling them as verified research.

## When to route
Use when Dan shares a substantive observation from leadership, student/family ministry, groups, one-on-one disciple-making, equipping, or church systems that should durably inform discipleship thinking, or explicitly asks to file a field note.

If the core artifact is a Bible passage study, use `discipleship-personal-scripture-study-capture`. If the input merely expresses a durable preference or theological conviction, use `discipleship-convictions-capture`. For an interview batch/measurement study requiring evidence analysis, route to the existing Southbrook qualitative diagnostic workflow instead. Do not capture ordinary factual queries or content Dan says not to store.

## What to preserve
Use Dan's voice and reasoning, date, stated context, concrete story as safely expressible, questions, uncertainty, and any proposed next steps. Only clean formatting artifacts; never silently substitute a polished AI theory for the source note. Do not flag, critique, or qualify the personal observation unless requested.

Separate minimal **metadata** from user-authored narrative:
- `DIRECT OBSERVATION`: something Dan reports personally witnessing (still Dan's observation, not independently verified).
- `REPORTED BY OTHERS`: firsthand account of someone else's report; avoid attributing certainty.
- `IMPRESSION / INTERPRETATION`: a proposed explanation, not a measured outcome.
- `IDEA / HYPOTHESIS`: a possible change or mechanism worth considering.
- `EXPERIMENT / LEARNING`: only when actual activity and outcomes are documented.

These classifications are provenance tags, not assessments of Dan's judgment. Preserve what's uncertain without unsolicited criticism.

## Steps
1. Decide whether there is a reusable original observation rather than generic chat; seek consent if it is ambiguous whether Dan wants a highly personal note filed.
2. Respect privacy and pastoral safeguarding: do not store confidential counseling, specific child/student identity, personnel performance information, abuse reports, private medical records, or unconsented identifiable interview detail in this general shared Brain. Where possible, create a fully de-identified mechanism-level summary **only with adequate permission**; otherwise stop without saving those details.
3. Search for a matching dated/topic note. Create a dated, topic-named native Google Doc or update the existing one if it is explicitly a continuation.
4. Preserve the original narrative separately from administrative indexing, identify whether observation/impression/idea when stated, and link related biblical study, Pastoral Lens, diagnostic, or pilot record only when specifically relevant.
5. Place under verified folder, reread body and parent location, and report URL and result.
6. Do not invoke research or pilot skills merely for recording a note. Only hand off if Dan requests comparison, diagnosis, test design, or formal decision-making.

## Future retrieval
Before a relevant strategy, pilot, or curriculum request, look up a **small** set of matching field notes. Attribute notes as Dan's observation, not independent outcome evidence. Compare to research/local diagnostics when asked, preserving any tensions without rewriting the originals.

## Acceptance tests
- "I've noticed group leaders rarely apprentice someone": capture Dan's field observation; no universal statistical claim.
- "What do group leaders typically do?": answer, no field note.
- Dan shares an identifiable student pastoral situation: do not save confidential details into general Brain.
- Dan says "Maybe we could try giving apprentice leaders one task a month": capture as tentative if clearly for durable Brain, not approved church strategy.
- Dan revises a previous observation: preserve provenance and date, update linked follow-up rather than overwrite history.
