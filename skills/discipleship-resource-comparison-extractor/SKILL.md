# Discipleship Resource Comparison & Extraction

## Purpose
Answer: **Across these actual resources, which components are best suited to the same capability, why, and what should we preserve without mechanically mixing conflicting methods?**

Distinct from `compare-big-picture` (research finding synthesis) and `discipleship-practice-intelligence-builder` (ministry practices/systems).

## Trigger
"Compare these worksheets," "which assignment is better for teaching __," "pull out the best exercises," "create a shortlist of resources for __." Require two or more sufficiently accessible actual artifacts or completed Resource Review Cards; don't invent comparative details.

## Procedure
1. Fix the **same local use case**: participant, starting capability, desired behavior, ministry setting, duration, coach/leader capacity, and desired evidence of transfer.
2. Keep user-specified source boundary. Retrieve existing Review Cards; evaluate unevaluated inputs narrowly as needed. Record `UNAVAILABLE / UNREVIEWED` instead of comparing from reputation.
3. Normalize comparisons to shared criteria from `discipleship-resource-evaluator` without flattening meaningful variations in theology, audience, pedagogy, or time commitment.
4. Extract *specific teaching moves* by source and locator: demonstrations, prompts, role-plays, feedback moves, take-home assignments, follow-ups, scaffolds, teach-back checks, debriefs. Note what is genuinely distinct vs repeated within one source family.
5. Identify why each component could support the capability, separating document observation, mechanism hypothesis, evidence, and Southbrook fit. A repeated pattern is not causal proof.
6. Examine **compatibility**: do their aims and sequences fit together? Would selecting pieces remove essential pastoral/theological context, safeguards, feedback, or coaching? Does a simpler coherent single-source approach beat the assembled alternative?
7. Explicitly preserve **rights**: `VERIFIED REUSABLE`, `PERMISSION NEEDED`, `ORIGINAL REDESIGN ONLY`, `UNKNOWN`. Do not assemble an unauthorized derivative or reproduce long protected exercises.
8. Rank *for the defined use case*, not globally. Recommend `USE ONE SOURCE`, `COMBINE COMPATIBLE PATTERNS IN ORIGINAL DESIGN`, `TEST TWO ALTERNATIVES`, `NEEDS MORE REVIEW`, or `NOT NOW`.
9. Name confidence/uncertainty, reasons an apparently strong option might fail, and exactly what local feedback could discriminate.
10. Handoff to `discipleship-training-tool-designer` only when creation is requested or a full resource chain includes authorized draft design. If user only requests comparison, stop.

## Output: Comparative Resource Matrix
Use `references/discipleship-resource-comparison-template.md`:
- Common equipping outcome and constraints
- Sources/versions/locators/rights/access coverage
- Criteria comparison with strongest evidence *from the resources*
- Strongest distinctive elements (with source IDs, not copied text)
- Incompatibilities and non-negotiable context
- Alternative candidate architectures including simple single-source choice
- Use-case recommendation with counterarguments
- Original-design guidance / permission needs
- Testable learning questions and next step
- Explicit review vs approval status

## Hand-off boundary
This skill extracts and prioritizes *patterns*, not public-ready copied teaching content. For complete multi-session curriculum design use `discipleship-curriculum-architect` / `full-discipleship-curriculum-build-cycle`. For new research questions use the separate research workflow.
