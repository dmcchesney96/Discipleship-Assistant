# Discipleship Resource Evaluator

## Purpose
Answer: **Is this particular resource fit for equipping disciples, which elements are strong or weak, and what would we need to change or test?**

Evaluate actual content and instructions, not merely a ministry's reputation, impact claims, or attractive visual design.

## Trigger
Use for "evaluate this worksheet," "review these assignments," "is this leader guide worth using," or "what should we keep/change about this exercise?" May be invoked by `full-discipleship-resource-equipping-cycle`.

## Required inputs and access gate
Retrieve the original artifact at sufficient resolution to inspect the relevant pages, questions, activity instructions, leader notes, and stated aims. Keep exact page/section/source locators. If only a review or description is accessible, report **INSUFFICIENT PRIMARY MATERIAL** and do not issue an item-level quality verdict. Retrieve the smallest relevant pastoral alignment lens for substantial local-use judgments; it is a preference input, not evidence of efficacy.

## Evaluate using six distinct lenses
Use concrete artifact evidence for each lens and mark `NOT ASSESSABLE` where necessary:

1. **Biblical and theological fidelity:** handling of passages in context; gospel/grace/obedience balance; doctrinal tensions; practices requiring pastoral oversight. Flag uncertain exegesis for human theological review; do not certify doctrine autonomously.
2. **Formation rather than information only:** does the learner personally engage Jesus/Scripture, reflection, repentance, prayer, obedience, relationships or mission appropriate to the stated purpose? A narrowly informational tool can be useful; don't force all dimensions into every exercise.
3. **Ordinary-person usability:** clarity, reading level, instructions, time, materials, access, cultural assumptions, realistic household/volunteer burden, accessibility, psychological safety.
4. **Transfer into actual capability:** modeling, guided rehearsal, authentic practice, feedback, follow-up, real-world application and ability to generalize to a new situation. Distinguish "talks about doing" from doing.
5. **Reproduction / ownership transfer:** can recipients eventually explain/model/teach an appropriate bounded practice without excessive staff dependence? Distinguish witness or facilitation from formal teaching/authority.
6. **Southbrook contextual relevance:** explicit local need, ministry setting, leader capacity, age/stage, group size, established language, pastoral preferences, approved decisions, safeguarding.

**Cross-cutting gates:** source integrity, usage rights/licensing, privacy, child protection, pastoral-care referral boundaries, accessibility, production/update cost. These gates are not averaged away by attractive scores.

## Rating discipline
For each of the six lenses, optional anchored rating: `0 = material flaw`, `1 = limited`, `2 = adequate`, `3 = especially strong`, or `NOT ASSESSABLE`. Quote or describe a **specific element and locator** supporting each rating. **Never sum to a universal "disciple-making effectiveness" score.** Distinguish:
- `CONTENT OBSERVATION` (visible in the artifact);
- `DESIGN JUDGMENT` (reasoned potential);
- `EFFECT EVIDENCE` (reported vs independently measured, if present);
- `LOCAL FIT ASSUMPTION` (requires diagnosis or test).

## Procedure
1. Define the intended audience, setting, action/skill, and resource purpose.
2. Read relevant original content and facilitator notes; record version/rights/access.
3. Explain what the participant actually does, during and after the session; identify how the facilitator supports and corrects.
4. Evaluate six lenses with evidence/locators and an explicit strongest counterargument to your recommendation.
5. Extract the best 1–3 specific, source-attributed *design patterns* without reproducing protected text.
6. Mark issues: must-correct theological/safeguarding/rights, improve-before-use, optional optimization, and uncertainties.
7. Give separate **resource recommendation**: `CONSIDER AS-IS (RIGHTS CHECK)`, `ADAPT IF PERMITTED`, `BORROW THE DESIGN PRINCIPLE / WRITE ORIGINAL`, `REVISE HEAVILY`, `NOT NOW`, or `NEEDS MORE INFORMATION`.
8. Suggest a smallest legitimate classroom/real-life test when requested; never invent observed impact or approval.

## Required output: Resource Review Card
Use `templates/discipleship-resource-review-card-template.md`.
- ID, source, exact locator, audience/version, rights and access status
- Stated vs inferred capability and practical session flow
- Six-lens findings with concrete examples/locators
- What is strong; what is missing; must-fix risks; tradeoffs
- Design patterns worth learning from
- Quality/effectiveness distinction and local-fit uncertainty
- Recommendation, rights condition, possible next step, disposition/status
- Links to Practice Intelligence / research only when relevant to a contested mechanism or factual claim

## Hard boundaries
- Printed polish, popularity, attendance, self-reported multiplication or a famous author are not proof of effectiveness.
- A `REVIEWED` resource is not automatically `APPROVED`.
- Do not reproduce or closely paraphrase a copyrighted worksheet for distribution when rights are unknown. For original writing, use high-level functional ideas and original wording; seek permission/licensed material for reuse/adaptation.
- Research verification is *conditional*: invoke `source-evidence-checker` for consequential external effect claims, not every exercise.
- Apply stricter human review for doctrinal teaching, minors, counseling, trauma, confidentiality, and pastoral authority.

## Stop
Stop at the evaluation requested; do not automatically construct an entire curriculum or create a Southbrook-branded handout.
