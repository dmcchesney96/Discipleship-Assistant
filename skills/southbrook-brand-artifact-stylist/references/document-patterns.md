## Observed visual hierarchy

The Booklet cover uses the full church identity, a prominent document title, ample white space, and a small revision line. Interior pages generally use strong charcoal sans-serif titles, thin cyan rules, and smaller category labels. Some secondary headings are bold italic and written as ordinary questions. Short labels are sometimes uppercase cyan; main titles are not uniformly all caps. Use a meaningful hierarchy rather than capitalizing everything.

The sampled pages' font metadata supports Gotham Book/Bold and italic variants, consistent with the existing profile. Use licensed Gotham when available and Arial as the editable fallback. Do not extract embedded fonts. Do not infer color tokens from raster screenshots; use the verified profile tokens.

Cyan works as a restrained accent in rules, selected title words, category labels, and key distinctions. Charcoal body copy and white space carry the reading experience. Keep small text sufficiently dark for contrast; the aim is readable Southbrook identity, not mechanical duplication of every sampled choice.

## Organize by the reader's task

Use short question/action headings that answer what the reader needs next. Observed examples include “How to Use This Guide,” a meeting's purpose before its agenda, and a practical instruction before a worksheet. The website similarly groups a brief explanation under recognizable categories and offers a fitting next step.

A useful adaptation for a learning resource is:

`Purpose → Understand the idea → See an example → Practice together → Try it in life → Help someone else begin`

This sequence is our synthesis of the examples and Dan's resource-design aim, not an official required Southbrook template. Scale it to the task. A short handout may need only three action sections and a next step. An evidence report still needs its source/argument structure.

## Patterns by artifact

- **Teaching/leader guide:** clear title; a short orientation; question or action headings; concise explanation; a worked example; usable prompts; a named next use. Put Scripture beside the practice it informs.
- **Workbook:** clearly marked week/unit, brief purpose, a few questions, and intentional response space. The sampled Booklet leaves substantial room for answers; do not fill all space with more teaching. Match space to the response task rather than adding blank pages as decoration.
- **Working agenda:** purpose and key facts first; numbered/time-bounded topics; outcome, useful questions, and decision/output; close with action, owner, and due date. This is an observed working pattern, not a requirement for devotional materials.
- **Comparison:** use common row labels and parallel columns when readers need to see a genuine distinction. Preserve enough white space and normal-size text.
- **Practice page:** show a completed example near the instructions, then invite an attempt. Keep follow-up and questions concrete. A simple repeated structure can help a person carry the practice without memorizing a manual.
- **Website/screen copy:** concise page/category headings, short paragraphs, scannable bullets, and a direct next-step label. Adapt this clarity to documents rather than copying website navigation or digital hero layouts into every handout.

## Review questions

Can the reader find the purpose, current section, and next action quickly? Are heading levels clear in both appearance and native styles? Is the material organized around its use? Does it leave enough space for a real response? Is the visual identity recognizably Southbrook without obscuring evidence, attribution, or status?
