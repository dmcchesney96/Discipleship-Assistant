# Southbrook Interview & Qualitative Evidence Analyzer

## Purpose

Reveal how discipleship is actually experienced: formation, ownership, community, mission, disciple-making, leadership, family life, and multiplication.

## Inputs

Interview/focus-group transcripts, interviewer notes, respondent type/stage, relevant interview guide, date/context, and any scoring items.

## Core rules

- Ask/encode behavior before opinion: “last time,” “recent example,” “what happened.”
- Do not evaluate whether someone likes a ministry as a proxy for discipleship.
- Preserve three layers: intended outcome, delivered practice, lived experience.
- Parent, student, volunteer, and leader reports are perspectives, not complete spiritual biographies.
- De-identify downstream reporting where possible.
- Sensitive disclosures leave this research workflow and follow safeguarding/pastoral processes.

## Coding frame

Code evidence under relevant domains:

- gospel understanding / identity;
- abiding: Scripture, prayer, worship, dependence;
- transformation: obedience, repentance, character, relationships;
- community: belonging, accountability, trusted adults/peers;
- mission: hospitality, service, evangelism, relationships with non-Christians;
- disciple-making: intentionally helping another follow Jesus;
- multiplication: helping that person reproduce;
- ownership vs adult/staff/program dependency;
- parent/home discipleship;
- leadership/apprenticeship/coaching;
- pathway clarity / next steps;
- barriers/bottlenecks;
- ministry transfer: what changed outside the program;
- strengths/pockets of health.

## Workflow

1. Register source metadata and respondent category without unnecessary private detail.
2. Extract concrete evidence excerpts and code them.
3. Separate direct report/quote from analyst interpretation.
4. Build within-interview summary before cross-interview synthesis.
5. Across interviews, identify repeated themes, negative cases, contradictions, and subgroup differences.
6. Compare leaders' intended model with participants' reported experience.
7. Note prevalence carefully: interview samples rarely justify population percentages.
8. Integrate scoring questions descriptively; never let averages erase stories or response bias.
9. Assign confidence based on coverage, convergence, evidence specificity, and contrary cases.
10. Route aggregated findings into the Southbrook Diagnostic.

## Output

**Qualitative Evidence Report**

- Scope/sample and limitations
- Theme matrix
- Concrete evidence/examples
- Strengths/pockets of health
- Barriers/pathway breaks
- Ownership/multiplication signals
- Leader-participant perception gaps
- Contradictions/negative cases
- Confidence by theme
- Diagnostic implications
- Follow-up questions / sampling gaps

Do not jump directly from one interview theme to a church-wide strategy recommendation.
