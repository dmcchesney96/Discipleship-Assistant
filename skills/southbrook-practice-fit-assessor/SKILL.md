# Southbrook Practice Fit Assessor

## Purpose

Answer:

> **Given what we know about Southbrook and these practices, which are worth exploring, testing, piloting, integrating, or setting aside — and why?**

Use `docs/research-practice-action-framework.md`.

This is not an empirical-effectiveness ranking. It is a **contextual action-warrant assessment**.

## Inputs

- specific Southbrook problem/opportunity;
- current-state / diagnostic evidence;
- Practice Cards or concrete practice descriptions;
- applicable Living Synthesis mechanisms/principles;
- biblical/theological constraints;
- relevant local capacity/leadership/ministry realities;
- known pilot/local learning;
- decision horizon and scale.

## Workflow

1. Define the local problem before evaluating solutions.
2. Retrieve current Southbrook reality and intended outcomes.
3. For each practice, evaluate these lenses separately:
   - biblical/theological coherence;
   - North Star contribution;
   - clarity of actual practice;
   - plausible mechanism;
   - direct effect evidence;
   - mechanism evidence;
   - practitioner precedent;
   - transferability;
   - fit with Southbrook's specific bottleneck;
   - ordinary-person simplicity/reproducibility;
   - coaching/support requirement;
   - staff dependence;
   - ministry/cultural compatibility;
   - capacity/cost;
   - risk/pastoral downside;
   - reversibility;
   - time to learn;
   - measurability;
   - multiplication/ownership potential.
4. Name missing information explicitly.
5. Apply an **evidence threshold proportional to the action**.
6. Do not disqualify a low-risk reversible test merely because exact-program causal evidence is thin.
7. Do not recommend broad integration merely because a practice is popular or intuitively appealing.
8. Prefer the smallest useful test when uncertainty is material.
9. Preserve meaningful alternatives; do not force a single winner when several practices solve different subproblems.

## Action-warrant statuses

Use one per practice:

- **EXPLORE**
- **SMALL-TEST CANDIDATE**
- **PILOT CANDIDATE**
- **INTEGRATION CANDIDATE**
- **SCALE CANDIDATE**
- **NOT NOW / POOR FIT**

Explain the warrant. Do not present it as universal evidence confidence.

## Decision-calibrated standard

### Small reversible test

Can be warranted by:
- sound theological fit;
- plausible mechanism;
- credible practitioner precedent or adjacent evidence;
- real local need;
- low downside;
- clear learning question.

### Bounded pilot

Add:
- operational clarity;
- sufficient training/coaching;
- reasonable measurement;
- manageable implementation burden;
- clear exit/adapt decision.

### Integration / scale

Require materially more:
- local pilot evidence;
- sustained or repeated learning;
- capacity;
- ordinary-person reproducibility;
- fidelity;
- understood side effects;
- leadership decision/authorization.

## Output

# Southbrook Practice Fit Assessment — [Problem]

## Local problem
What is actually happening and why it matters.

## What the strongest current research says
Only the mechanism/principle layer relevant to the choice.

## Practice comparison

For each practice:
- practice summary;
- strongest reasons it may fit;
- strongest reasons it may not fit;
- direct evidence;
- mechanism evidence;
- local-fit considerations;
- staff/reproducibility considerations;
- risk/reversibility;
- action warrant;
- what must be learned next.

## Best next learning moves

Identify 1–3 bounded next moves, not a giant strategy.

## What should not be decided yet

Name premature conclusions.

## Handoff

- SMALL TEST / PILOT → `discipleship-experiment-pilot-designer`
- leadership choice → `strategy-decision-brief-builder`
- insufficient practice detail → `discipleship-practice-intelligence-builder`
- missing local reality → `southbrook-discipleship-diagnostic`

A fit assessment is not an approved decision.
