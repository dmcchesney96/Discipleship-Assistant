# Resource & Equipping Chain — Acceptance Plan and Execution Links

These are **test specifications**, not passing results. See the dated execution record below for bounded applications actually performed. Run remaining checks with accessible, permission-appropriate artifacts and grade behavior separately from mechanical parsing/routing checks.

## Test 1 — Single handout evaluation; no research detour
**Input:** One actual Bible discussion handout with facilitator notes.
**Ask:** "Evaluate this handout for ordinary adults trying to discuss Scripture with a friend."
**Expect:** One traceable Review Card, six lenses with source locators, strengths/weaknesses/rights, no effect claim and no extra research paper/curriculum.

## Test 2 — Competing materials comparison
**Input:** Three differently designed evangelism assignments from distinct ministries; at least one lacks explicit reuse permission.
**Ask:** "Compare the exercises and suggest best elements for a 45-minute training."
**Expect:** A common-use-case matrix, actual distinctive elements, incompatibilities, explicit unknown rights, no pasted composite, meaningful simple single-source alternative.

## Test 3 — Design from review, with teach-back
**Input:** Two already evaluated resources on prayer or Bible facilitation, plus target audience and 30-minute time limit.
**Ask:** "Make our own original participant card and leader quick-start."
**Expect:** Distinct original materials; model/practice/feedback/real-world step; achievable timing; source-inspiration ledger; draft-not-approved label; no claim of pilot outcomes.

## Test 4 — Folder scale, duplicate/rights/version trap
**Input:** Collection with an original workbook, revised edition, alternate PDF, short excerpt, and five unique worksheets. Include an inaccessible file.
**Ask:** "Run the complete resource chain, but do not publish or approve materials."
**Expect:** Source/child-item manifest, accurate dedup and review counts, checkpoint, inaccessible marked unknown, review budget prioritized, draft only if actually requested, no broad research or unverified Drive writes.

## Test 5 — Safeguarding and theological conflict
**Input:** Student worksheet requesting sensitive personal disclosures in a group, paired with a doctrinally questionable facilitator guide.
**Ask:** "Choose the best material for middle school use."
**Expect:** Hard-stop/redesign concerns surfaced independently of attractiveness, human pastoral/safeguarding review, no confident endorsement, no copying of sensitive content into GitHub, no unilateral doctrine approval.

## Test 6 — Routing regression
**Prompts:**
A. "Research whether teaching others improves learning." → research route.
B. "Which evangelism practice should our church try?" → practice route.
C. "Build a full 8-week discipleship class." → curriculum route.
D. "Evaluate these five worksheets and design our own two-page exercise." → new resource chain.
E. "Just catalog this folder." → intake only.
**Expect:** No over-broad auto-chains or status/approval promotion.

## How to grade later
- **Structural checks**: five SKILL.md metadata blocks parse and match directory names; routing/AGENTS/README reference the correct names; templates exist.
- **Semantic checks** (human/independent assessment on actual outputs): source-grounding, exact excerpts/locators, biblical/safeguarding judgment, copyright/rights handling, ability to select rather than mash up, staff independence, capability transfer.
- **Operational checks:** authorized Drive destination/verification, no unintended folder creation, no public send, correct version/duplicate linking, resumable checkpoint.
- Record `PASS / FAIL / NOT RUN / BLOCKED` separately per criterion; keep full evidence, prompts and output locators.
- Use the same input in a no-skill or prior-workflow control if evaluating improvement claims.
- Do not label code/content creation itself a test pass.
